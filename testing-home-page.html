Aussie Broadband

Compliance uplift

Reduced a 17-module compliance suite to 12 meaningful, role-relevant modules — replacing scroll-and-click acknowledgement with genuine knowledge checks, and drawing praise from external auditors for an approach well ahead of industry standard.

Learning Design Compliance Workday Articulate 360 UX

The challenge

Completion meant nothing. There was no way to know if anyone had actually learned anything.

The compliance suite was legislatively grounded — that wasn't the problem. The problem was that modules relied on scroll-and-acknowledge: pages of content people could skip past, hit the button, and be marked complete. With no knowledge checks, the business had no evidence of understanding. Completion data existed. Comprehension data didn't.

What I did

Built a compliance suite that could prove its own value.

I reviewed every module against the legislation it claimed to address, removing those without a clear regulatory basis. Retained modules were converted to either a question-first approach — resources as PDFs with aligned quizzes — or an adaptive approach where content is only served on topics where staff answer incorrectly. I consolidated five campaigns into one, rewired Workday reporting for clean end-of-year tracking, and ran a full gap analysis for Symbio staff post-acquisition. SME and Risk & Compliance feedback was incorporated iteratively, with quarterly updates to the ELT.

"Is there legislation, a standard or ISO requirement for this training? If not, remove the module and make the information accessible via the intranet."

— The filter applied to every module in the review

The outcome

Auditors called it well ahead of industry standard.

Staff reported that their time felt meaningfully spent. Errors and broken links began surfacing through proper feedback channels rather than Slack DMs. External auditors specifically noted the data-driven approach to knowledge assessment — including measurable improvement in recall for safety, security, and ER procedures — as significantly ahead of most businesses they reviewed. Phase 2 is underway: upgraded security awareness training and new DFSV modules aligned to the updated ACMA standard.

Aussie Broadband — Security Operations

Learning Hour with SecOps

Designed a lightweight fortnightly learning system for the Security Operations team — turning ad hoc professional development into a structured, trackable commitment that changed how the team thought about learning at work.

L&D Systems SharePoint Agile Knowledge Management VIA Strengths

The challenge

Security staff wanted to learn more consistently. They just had no system to hold themselves to it.

After running a VIA Strengths session with the SecOps team, a clear theme emerged: they wanted to be more intentional and accountable about professional development. The desire was there. What was missing was a simple structure — something lightweight enough to fit inside two-week sprints, but rigorous enough to create real commitment and build a useful knowledge base over time.

What I did

Designed a three-phase system built around the team's existing way of working.

Working with the team, I designed a commit–complete–reflect loop aligned to their Agile sprint cadence. At the start of each fortnight, staff made a learning commitment. On completing it, they reflected on what they'd learned. Those reflections fed a growing SharePoint knowledge base — curated lists of recommended learning for analysts, engineers, leaders and aspiring security professionals — drawing from LinkedIn Learning, Microsoft Learn, Grafana, Atlassian and more. I built the SharePoint forms, trained the team, supported staff who weren't sure what to pursue, and handed ownership to the leaders after three months of hypercare.

Three phases: Commitment → Completing → Reflection. Simple enough to run inside a sprint. Substantial enough to build something lasting.

The outcome

A knowledge base that kept growing after I stepped back.

The system runs independently, owned by the team and its leaders. The SharePoint houses a growing curated library of recommendations — useful for existing staff and for anyone looking to move into security and governance. Most importantly, the team's attitude towards professional development shifted: learning stopped being something that happened when absolutely necessary and became a regular, meaningful part of how they work.

Projects with Monash University & UQ

Earlier in my career I worked as an Educational Designer across the Faculty of Arts at Monash and the Faculty of Medicine at UQ — building tools, designing curriculum, and solving problems that ranged from responsive HTML to automating an entire performance assessment workflow during COVID.

Monash University — Faculty of Arts

ATS1665: Understanding Gender 1

Co-designed a first-of-its-kind fully online undergraduate unit during COVID lockdowns — achieving the highest student rating for both a first-time unit coordinator and any Arts Faculty unit delivered entirely online.

Online Learning Moodle H5P Mahara Assessment Design

The challenge

Design the ideal online unit — and set the template for a new generation of Arts offerings.

When COVID lockdowns hit, the Faculty of Arts faced a hard problem: students couldn't attend face-to-face classes but needed high-quality alternatives. The Associate Dean of Arts directed our team to create a flagship online unit that would demonstrate what excellent online learning in Arts could look like — and serve as a model for others to follow. The unit coordinator, Frankie Egan, was coordinating for the first time. The bar was high and the timeline was tight.

What I did

Built a highly structured, visually rich unit with three levels of depth per week.

I worked closely with Frankie to design the full assessment schedule, teaching content and student communication strategy. Every week of content was built at three levels — basic, intermediate and advanced — giving students genuine choice in how deeply they engaged. The unit ran ten weekly lessons plus live guest lectures and Q&A sessions (extended from two to four guests). I trained Frankie on all educational technology, monitored weekly progress, and documented the process into a reusable template for future units.

The outcome

Highest rating for a first-time coordinator. Highest rating for an online-only Arts unit.

The unit received outstanding student feedback — ranking as the highest-rated unit for a first-time coordinator and the highest-rated fully online unit in the Faculty of Arts. Students responded positively to the layered content approach and the interactive assessment design: four quizzes (top two scores counted), weekly H5P activities, six Mahara blog posts building to a final essay.

Monash University — Faculty of Arts / IT

BEEST

Conceived and co-built a tool that let non-technical academics create responsive, Bootstrap-powered HTML elements for Moodle — used by 500+ staff and influential enough that the central teaching and learning unit built their own version into Moodle's backend.

Tool Design HTML / CSS / JS Bootstrap Moodle UX Research Google Analytics

The challenge

Academics could build content. They couldn't make it look good or work on mobile.

Moodle's default content tools produced static, desktop-only pages. Creating responsive, visually engaging elements required HTML skills most academics didn't have — and weren't going to learn. The result was a platform full of content that was hard to read, broken on mobile, and inconsistent in quality. Educational Designers spent significant time either building elements from scratch or trying to teach HTML to people who didn't want to learn it.

What I did

Built the first element, then convinced two expert designers it was worth building the rest.

I built a basic button creator using my then-limited HTML, CSS and JavaScript knowledge — enough to demonstrate the concept. I used it to convince Matt Chen and Philip Abramson in the IT Faculty (a former front-end designer and a former back-end CMS expert) that the idea was worth their time. From there I led the UX: iterating the interface through multiple rounds of feedback, proposing the save-and-reuse feature, running four focus groups across Educational Designers, academics, students and senior T&L staff, recording all screencasts, writing all user guides, and setting up Google Analytics to track adoption.

First public semester: used over 1,000 times. Only 4 support inquiries. Only 6 instances of misuse found across 50 audited sites.

— Google Analytics audit

The outcome

500+ users. And the central unit built their own version because of it.

BEEST is still in active use by over 500 academics and professional staff across Monash. Every element it produces is fully responsive across desktop, tablet and mobile. Storage is handled entirely in the browser's local storage — no server costs, up to 10,000 items per user. The central teaching and learning unit saw the adoption figures and the self-sufficiency of users, and built a native version directly into Moodle's backend — the clearest possible validation that the concept worked.

University of Queensland — Faculty of Medicine

MD Design Project

Part of a team redesigning UQ's postgraduate medicine program from 32 individual courses into four integrated year-long programs — closing the gap between compartmentalised knowledge and the holistic clinical thinking real doctors need.

Curriculum Design Assessment Design Higher Education Bootstrap LearnX

The challenge

Students were learning in silos. Doctors don't work in silos.

Doctors in the field and current students raised a consistent concern: the existing 32-course structure produced graduates who could recall knowledge by subject but struggled to apply it holistically in clinical situations. UQ's Faculty of Medicine responded with an ambitious redesign — compressing the program into four integrated year-long courses, combining the themes, content and assessment of eight 12-week courses into each 32-week course.

What I did

Led learning design across five program streams, and brought responsive design to UQ's platform.

I worked across the Assessment Working Group and five content streams: Clinical and Practical Skills, Advocacy and Public Health, Ethics and Law, and Rural and Remote Medicine. Alongside the curriculum work, I applied what I'd built at Monash with BEEST to UQ's LearnX platform — designing the MEDI7100 Code Centre, a custom web application that added Bootstrap, Font Awesome and custom CSS to the platform, enabling fully responsive course content across devices. I also began developing professional development for academic and professional staff so they could build to the same standard without needing to code.

MEDI7100 Code Centre

The outcome

Scepticism turned to engagement. Students are actively shaping their own program.

Initial resistance from academics and student representatives — concerns about chaos and compromised standards — gave way to acceptance and then genuine enthusiasm after 18 months of planning and 10 weeks of delivery. The project team's close feedback loop with students meant content and assessment issues were resolved in hours rather than weeks. Students saw their feedback acted on and kept contributing. The voices calling for a return to the old model went quiet.

Monash University — Faculty of Arts / IT

Music performance in the pandemic

Co-built a fully automated Google Suite system that kept music and theatre performance assessment running through COVID lockdowns — preventing course cancellations, protecting casual staff jobs, and creating a reusable process for future disruptions.

Automation Google Apps Script Process Design Google Suite

The challenge

Music students can't perform in a band remotely. Or so everyone assumed.

When Monash sent everyone home, music and drama courses faced a unique crisis. Assessment depended on live performance — students forming bands, playing together, being watched and marked in real time. With no ability to share physical space, the question was existential: could these courses continue at all? The answer couldn't just be "move to Zoom" — it needed to handle submissions, marker assignment, permissions management and feedback distribution at scale, across six performance units and around 200 students.

What I did

Wireframed the entire process, then automated it end-to-end with Google Apps Script.

I designed the full online assessment workflow — from repertoire submission through to marks delivery — and presented it to the Associate Dean for Education, Dean of the Music School and leader of Teaching Support before a line of code was written. Working with Matt Chen from the IT Faculty, we used Google Forms, Drive and Apps Script to automate the entire pipeline: students submitted repertoire; Drive auto-created folders per student and piece; students received upload links; on the submission deadline, marker permissions were automatically injected into the correct folders; markers received briefing documents; after marking closed, students received links to their marks and feedback. Manual overrides were available for extensions at every stage.

The outcome

Courses ran. Jobs were protected. Two people did the work of a whole team.

Courses continued without delay. Casual and contract staff kept their jobs. All stakeholders gained new skills in recording and performing collaboratively online. The Faculty gained a reusable, largely automated process for any future disruption to live performance. Quantitative student feedback was slightly down from pre-pandemic years — expected given the circumstances — but qualitative feedback was overwhelmingly grateful. Staff were astonished that two people had automated what should have required a dedicated team. As a musician myself, this is one I'm particularly proud of.

Research

Both papers were completed as part of my Master of Education (Digital Learning Specialisation) at Monash, awarded with the Dean's Award for Academic Excellence.

Monash University — Master of Education

Building a quality balance in teaching and research

Explored why Australian higher education consistently prioritises research over teaching quality — and what a rigorous external ranking system for teaching could look like if universities chose to build one.

Higher education in Australia has a research ranking problem — not because ranking systems are wrong, but because only research has one. This paper traces the history that led to the current imbalance, examines what makes research rankings credible, and argues that a similarly rigorous independent framework for teaching and learning quality is both achievable and overdue. The main limitation: financial reporting in higher education makes it very hard to isolate what is actually being spent on teaching versus research.

Monash University — Master of Education

Experience and reflection: learning in action

Examined how experiential learning and community service learning models could be applied to improve outcomes for international students in Australia — specifically around language acquisition and workplace exploitation.

International students in Australia face two compounding problems: language development stalls after enrolment due to competing pressures, and unfamiliarity with Australian systems leaves many vulnerable to workplace exploitation. This paper proposes a mentoring and language exchange program — pairing new international students with those two or more years into their stay — as a practical, low-cost intervention that addresses both.
